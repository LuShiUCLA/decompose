# Why this lab works this way

Each design choice below names the behavioural finding it relies on, the source, and what the source does not show. The lab itself has not been evaluated in a trial; these are reasons for the design, not evidence that the lab works.

## Design choices and their evidence

| What the lab does                                                            | Finding it relies on                                                                                                    | Source                                           | Limit                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| One specific, hard goal: reproduce the headline within ±0.1 pp and defend it | Specific, difficult goals lead to higher performance than “do your best”                                                | Locke & Latham, 2002                             | Mostly shown for effort-driven tasks; for complex new tasks, learning goals can matter more than performance goals (discussed in the same review)                                                        |
| Two days with a night in between; recall questions return to earlier modules | Distributed practice improves retention relative to massed practice                                                     | Cepeda et al., 2006                              | The best gap depends on how long you need to remember; the review found that the best gap grows with the retention interval, so a one-day gap is a choice for a two-day lab, not an optimum              |
| Sleep between Day 1 and Day 2                                                | Sleep after learning supports memory consolidation                                                                      | Diekelmann & Born, 2010                          | Evidence is mainly from laboratory memory tasks, not from learning statistics                                                                                                                            |
| Quizzes after each idea, with recall of earlier modules                      | Taking a test improves later retention more than restudying (the testing effect)                                        | Roediger & Karpicke, 2006                        | Shown with prose passages and delayed tests; the lab's quizzes are short and multiple-choice                                                                                                             |
| Predict before seeing (the split, logit vs LPM, sandbox step 1)              | Attempting an answer, even unsuccessfully, before seeing it can enhance later learning                                  | Kornell, Hays & Bjork, 2009; Bjork & Bjork, 2020 | Shown with verbal materials (word pairs, trivia); applying it to predicting a quantitative result is an extrapolation. The benefit depends on feedback following the attempt, which the lab always gives |
| Faded worked examples in Module 2                                            | Moving from worked examples to partly completed ones to independent problems supports the transition to problem solving | Renkl et al., 2002                               | Studied with novices; if you already know Oaxaca–Blinder, skip to Step 3                                                                                                                                 |
| One piece of the formula per screen; no simultaneous text-plus-formula walls | Working memory holds only a few chunks; problem solving without guidance loads it heavily                               | Cowan, 2001; Sweller, 1988                       | Capacity estimates are for unrelated items; the screen size here is a judgement, not a calculation                                                                                                       |
| Words next to the numbers they explain; no decorative media                  | Evidence-based principles for reducing extraneous load in multimedia learning                                           | Mayer & Moreno, 2003; Mayer, 2008                | Principles were tested mostly with short science explanations                                                                                                                                            |
| Write the question and the results in your own words                         | Students who explain examples to themselves learn more from them                                                        | Chi et al., 1989                                 | Correlational in the original study; the lab's keyword checklist cannot judge quality, so it shows a model answer                                                                                        |
| Feedback says what was right, why, and the smallest next step                | Feedback about the task and the process is more effective than praise                                                   | Hattie & Timperley, 2007                         | Effects vary widely across studies                                                                                                                                                                       |
| A reproduction checkpoint you can pass                                       | Mastery experiences are a strong source of self-efficacy                                                                | Bandura, 1977                                    | Self-efficacy is not competence; the seminar questions test the latter                                                                                                                                   |

**What would count against the lab's use of these findings.** If learners who complete the lab reproduced the headline, diagnosed the sandbox cases and answered the seminar questions no better than learners who read the same material without the quizzes, predictions, fading and checkpoint, these design choices would not be earning their place. The lab has not been tested this way; a two-arm comparison on those three outcomes is the test.

## What the lab deliberately does not do

- **No “learning styles”.** A review found no adequate evidence that matching instruction to a learner's preferred style improves learning (Pashler et al., 2008), so the lab offers one path for everyone.
- **No neuroscience claims.** The lab's predict-first prompts rest on the behavioural evidence above, not on brain findings.
- **No tracking.** Progress is stored only in your browser (localStorage). Nothing you type, including the files you load into the exporter, leaves your computer.
- **No grades.** The checklist feedback is keyword-based and transparent; the model answers are for comparison, not for marking.
