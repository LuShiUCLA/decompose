Why two different gaps? On all children with an answer, the gap is − = points. The decomposition can only use children with every covariate observed. On those children the rates are % and %, so the gap is points. Children with a missing answer are not a random subset, so the two gaps differ. Always report which sample a number comes from.

Who is in each group

The grouping variable is HHLANGUAGE at baseline (2018/2019): “What is the primary language spoken in the household?” 1 = English, 2 = Spanish, 3 = Other language. Spanish and other languages are combined as “other-language” for the decomposition (only such children have complete data).

This is household language. It is not the child's bilingualism, not the parent's English proficiency, and not ethnicity. In 2024, NSCH-LC paper questionnaires were available in English and Spanish, and telephone assistance in English, Spanish, Mandarin, Russian, Hindi and Portuguese (Methodology Report).

What would “explaining” the gap mean?

The two groups differ in many measured ways: age, income, caregiver-reported attention items, access to care. A decomposition asks a counterfactual accounting question: if the other-language group had the English group's distribution of those measured characteristics, and those characteristics related to reported diagnosis the way they do among English-language households, how much of the gap would remain?

Compositional, not causal. The answer is arithmetic about group differences in measured characteristics. It does not say that any characteristic causes diagnosis, and it does not say what would happen if anyone intervened.

Write the question in your own words

A model answer

“How much smaller would the gap in caregiver-reported ADHD diagnosis between English- and other-language households be if other-language children had the same distribution of measured demographics, reported attention items, behaviours and access to care as English-language children, holding the English group's associations fixed?”

Why “Do other-language children have less ADHD?” is the wrong question

  1. The outcome is a report of having been told about a diagnosis, not ADHD itself. Lower reported diagnosis is compatible with less disorder, less recognition, less access, different reporting, or all of these.
  2. A decomposition does not estimate how common a disorder is; it partitions a difference between two observed means.
  3. Even a fully “explained” gap is not a causal answer: the covariates are associations, and some (the attention items) are caregiver reports that may themselves differ by language.

Why write it: explaining material to yourself is associated with better learning from examples (Chi et al., 1989). The checklist is keyword-based and transparent; compare your text with the model answer yourself.
