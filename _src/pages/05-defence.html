Compositional, not causal. The explained part is the share of the gap that lines up with differences in measured characteristics. It does not say those characteristics cause diagnosis, and the unexplained part is not “discrimination” or “under-diagnosis”: it is everything the model does not measure, including differences in reporting.

In plain words. Row 1 is this lab's headline. Row 2 changes one coding rule: when a second caregiver lives in the home but did not answer the mental-health question, the earlier rule assumes “not poor” instead of leaving the child out. Row 3 also takes two shortcuts: it averages the six income imputations before fitting, and it uses a simpler bootstrap. Row 3 reproduces the brief's figures exactly, intervals included. The shortcuts are why the brief's intervals cannot be matched by rows 1 and 2: they are computed differently, not wrongly typed. If your numbers match row 2 or row 3, your code works; you made the earlier choices.

The caregiver rule moves the sample by − = children and the explained part by most of a point (demographics block → pp). Averaging the imputations moves the points in the second decimal but drops the imputation uncertainty; drawing nh rather than nh−1 households without rescaling tends to understate the variance when strata hold few households. None of these is an arithmetic error. The lab uses the reviewed choices because they do not guess an unanswered caregiver’s health and they carry every source of uncertainty. E-values (VanderWeele & Ding, 2017): and for the point estimates; and for the confidence limits closest to 1. An E-value is the minimum strength of association, on the risk-ratio scale, that an unmeasured confounder would need with both language group and new reports, beyond the measured covariates, to fully explain away the observed risk ratio.

These model results are read from the logged pipeline output (data-notes/logged_reference/), not recomputed by the lab's programs; the descriptive shares above are recomputed by decomposition_lab_core.py.

Not causal either. A large E-value says how strong hidden confounding would have to be; it does not show that there is none, and it says nothing about whether reporting, recognition or access is the mechanism.
