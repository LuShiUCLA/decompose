Why this lab works this way

Each design choice below names the behavioural finding it relies on, the source, and what the source does not show. The lab itself has not been evaluated in a trial; these are reasons for the design, not evidence that the lab works.

Design choices and their evidence

What the lab doesFinding it relies onSourceLimit
One specific, hard goal: reproduce the headline within ±0.1 pp and defend itSpecific, difficult goals lead to higher performance than “do your best”Locke & Latham, 2002Mostly shown for effort-driven tasks; for complex new tasks, learning goals can matter more than performance goals (discussed in the same review)
Two days with a night in between; recall questions return to earlier modulesDistributed practice improves retention relative to massed practiceCepeda et al., 2006The best gap depends on how long you need to remember; the review found that the best gap grows with the retention interval, so a one-day gap is a choice for a two-day lab, not an optimum
Sleep between Day 1 and Day 2Sleep after learning supports memory consolidationDiekelmann & Born, 2010Evidence is mainly from laboratory memory tasks, not from learning statistics
Quizzes after each idea, with recall of earlier modulesTaking a test improves later retention more than restudying (the testing effect)Roediger & Karpicke, 2006Shown with prose passages and delayed tests; the lab's quizzes are short and multiple-choice
Predict before seeing (the split, logit vs LPM, sandbox step 1)Attempting an answer, even unsuccessfully, before seeing it can enhance later learningKornell, Hays & Bjork, 2009; Bjork & Bjork, 2020Shown with verbal materials (word pairs, trivia); applying it to predicting a quantitative result is an extrapolation. The benefit depends on feedback following the attempt, which the lab always gives
Faded worked examples in Module 2Moving from worked examples to partly completed ones to independent problems supports the transition to problem solvingRenkl et al., 2002Studied with novices; if you already know Oaxaca–Blinder, skip to Step 3
One piece of the formula per screen; no simultaneous text-plus-formula wallsWorking memory holds only a few chunks; problem solving without guidance loads it heavilyCowan, 2001; Sweller, 1988Capacity estimates are for unrelated items; the screen size here is a judgement, not a calculation
Words next to the numbers they explain; no decorative mediaEvidence-based principles for reducing extraneous load in multimedia learningMayer & Moreno, 2003; Mayer, 2008Principles were tested mostly with short science explanations
Write the question and the results in your own wordsStudents who explain examples to themselves learn more from themChi et al., 1989Correlational in the original study; the lab's keyword checklist cannot judge quality, so it shows a model answer
Feedback says what was right, why, and the smallest next stepFeedback about the task and the process is more effective than praiseHattie & Timperley, 2007Effects vary widely across studies
A reproduction checkpoint you can passMastery experiences are a strong source of self-efficacyBandura, 1977Self-efficacy is not competence; the seminar questions test the latter

What would count against the lab's use of these findings. If learners who complete the lab reproduced the headline, diagnosed the sandbox cases and answered the seminar questions no better than learners who read the same material without the quizzes, predictions, fading and checkpoint, these design choices would not be earning their place. The lab has not been tested this way; a two-arm comparison on those three outcomes is the test.

What the lab deliberately does not do

  • No “learning styles”. A review found no adequate evidence that matching instruction to a learner's preferred style improves learning (Pashler et al., 2008), so the lab offers one path for everyone.
  • No neuroscience claims. The lab's predict-first prompts rest on the behavioural evidence above, not on brain findings.
  • No tracking. Progress is stored only in your browser (localStorage). Nothing you type, including the files you load into the exporter, leaves your computer.
  • No grades. The checklist feedback is keyword-based and transparent; the model answers are for comparison, not for marking.
